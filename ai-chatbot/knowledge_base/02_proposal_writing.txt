TITLE: FYP Proposal Writing Guide

WHAT IS AN FYP PROPOSAL?
An FYP proposal is a formal document that outlines your proposed project, its significance, methodology, and expected outcomes. It serves as a contract between you and your supervisor about what you plan to accomplish.

PROPOSAL STRUCTURE
A standard FYP proposal should include the following sections:

1. TITLE
- Should be clear, specific, and descriptive
- Avoid overly broad or vague titles
- Include the main technology or approach (e.g., "A Machine Learning-Based Approach for...")
- Typically 10-20 words

2. BACKGROUND / INTRODUCTION
- Introduce the problem domain
- Explain why this area is important
- Provide context for the reader
- Should be 1-2 paragraphs

3. PROBLEM STATEMENT
- Clearly define the specific problem you are addressing
- Explain what gap exists in current solutions
- State who is affected by this problem
- Should be precise and focused

4. OBJECTIVES
- List 3-5 specific, measurable objectives
- Use action verbs: "To design...", "To develop...", "To evaluate..."
- Each objective should be achievable within the FYP timeframe
- Objectives should be SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound

5. SCOPE
- Define what your project WILL cover
- Clearly state what is OUT of scope
- Set boundaries for the project
- Mention any assumptions or constraints

6. LITERATURE REVIEW (Summary)
- Review 5-10 relevant existing solutions or research papers
- Compare and contrast existing approaches
- Identify gaps that your project will address
- Use proper citations (IEEE or APA format)

7. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY
- Describe your development approach (Agile, Waterfall, Prototyping, etc.)
- Explain the tools and technologies you will use
- Outline the steps you will follow
- Mention data collection methods if applicable

8. EXPECTED OUTCOMES
- List the deliverables of your project
- Describe the expected results
- Mention how you will evaluate success

9. PROJECT TIMELINE
- Provide a Gantt chart or timeline table
- Include key milestones for both FYP1 and FYP2
- Be realistic about time allocation

10. REFERENCES
- Use IEEE or APA citation format (check with your programme)
- Include at least 5-10 references
- Prefer recent publications (within 5 years)
- Use academic sources: journals, conferences, books

COMMON MISTAKES IN PROPOSALS
- Too broad scope: trying to solve too many problems
- Vague objectives: not specific or measurable
- Missing methodology: not explaining HOW you will do the work
- No literature review: not showing awareness of existing work
- Unrealistic timeline: underestimating time needed
- Poor writing: grammar errors, unclear sentences
- No references: lack of academic sources

TIPS FOR A GOOD PROPOSAL
- Start early and iterate
- Discuss your idea with your supervisor before writing
- Use clear, academic language
- Be specific about technologies and tools
- Show awareness of limitations
- Proofread carefully
- Follow the faculty template if provided
