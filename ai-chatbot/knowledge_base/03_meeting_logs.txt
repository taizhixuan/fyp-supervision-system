TITLE: Meeting Logs and Supervision Documentation

WHAT ARE MEETING LOGS?
Meeting logs (also called supervision logs or logbooks) are formal records of supervision meetings between students and their supervisors. At MMU FCI, meeting logs follow a standard format and serve as evidence of regular supervision throughout the FYP.

PURPOSE OF MEETING LOGS
- Document discussions and decisions made during meetings
- Track project progress over time
- Record feedback and action items
- Serve as evidence for assessment
- Help both student and supervisor stay aligned on project goals

MMU FCI MEETING LOG FORMAT
Each meeting log should include:

1. HEADER INFORMATION
- Student name and ID
- Supervisor name
- Meeting date and time
- Meeting number (sequential)
- Project title

2. DISCUSSION SUMMARY
- Key topics discussed during the meeting
- Decisions made
- Feedback from supervisor
- Questions raised and answers provided

3. WORK DONE (Since Last Meeting)
- Progress made since the previous meeting
- Tasks completed
- Deliverables submitted
- Any challenges encountered

4. WORK TO BE DONE (Before Next Meeting)
- Specific tasks assigned
- Deadlines for each task
- Resources needed
- Priority of tasks

5. PROBLEMS ENCOUNTERED AND SOLUTIONS
- Technical or non-technical issues faced
- How they were resolved or plans to resolve them
- Help needed from supervisor

6. SIGNATURES
- Student signature with date
- Supervisor signature with date
- Both parties must sign to validate the log

BEST PRACTICES FOR MEETING LOGS
- Prepare before each meeting: bring progress, questions, and issues
- Write the log immediately after the meeting while details are fresh
- Be specific and detailed in your notes
- Keep a professional tone
- Submit logs promptly for supervisor review
- Keep copies of all signed logs

MEETING FREQUENCY
- Minimum: once every two weeks during active FYP semesters
- Recommended: weekly meetings, especially during implementation phase
- Students should schedule meetings proactively
- Notify supervisor in advance if you cannot attend

TIPS FOR PRODUCTIVE SUPERVISION MEETINGS
- Come prepared with an agenda
- Show tangible progress (demos, code, documents)
- Be honest about challenges and difficulties
- Take notes during the meeting
- Follow up on action items before the next meeting
- Respect your supervisor's time
