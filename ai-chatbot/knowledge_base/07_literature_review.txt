TITLE: Literature Review Guide

PURPOSE OF LITERATURE REVIEW
A literature review demonstrates your understanding of existing research and solutions in your project domain. It helps you:
- Identify what has been done before
- Find gaps that your project can address
- Understand the theoretical foundation
- Justify your approach
- Learn from others' methodologies

HOW TO CONDUCT A LITERATURE REVIEW

Step 1: Define Your Search Strategy
- Identify key terms related to your topic
- Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT)
- Search in academic databases: IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, Google Scholar, ScienceDirect, SpringerLink
- Focus on papers from the last 5 years

Step 2: Select Relevant Sources
- Read abstracts first to assess relevance
- Prioritize peer-reviewed journal articles and conference papers
- Include a mix of foundational and recent works
- Aim for 15-25 sources for your FYP
- Include sources about: similar systems, technologies used, methodologies

Step 3: Read and Analyze
- Read critically, not passively
- Note the problem each paper addresses
- Understand the methodology used
- Evaluate the results and limitations
- Consider how each relates to your project

Step 4: Organize Your Review
Options for organization:
- Chronological: trace the development of the field over time
- Thematic: group by themes or subtopics
- Methodological: group by research methods used
- Comparative: compare and contrast different approaches

Step 5: Write the Review
- Don't just summarize each paper individually
- Synthesize findings across multiple sources
- Identify patterns, agreements, and contradictions
- Clearly state the gap your project addresses
- Include a comparison table

COMPARISON TABLE
A comparison table is essential in FYP literature reviews. Include:
- Author and year
- System/approach name
- Key features
- Technology used
- Strengths
- Limitations
- Relevance to your project

Example columns: Reference | System Name | Features | Technology | Strengths | Weaknesses

WRITING TIPS FOR LITERATURE REVIEW
- Use present tense for general facts: "Machine learning is widely used..."
- Use past tense for specific studies: "Smith et al. (2023) proposed..."
- Don't copy text from sources (paraphrase and cite)
- Include critical analysis, not just description
- Connect each source to your project
- Transition smoothly between topics
- End with a summary of the gap your project fills

COMMON MISTAKES
- Just listing summaries without analysis
- Using only web sources (blogs, Wikipedia)
- Not citing sources properly
- Reviewing too few sources
- Not identifying a clear research gap
- Including irrelevant sources
- Copying text without proper citation (plagiarism)

CITATION MANAGEMENT TOOLS
- Mendeley (free)
- Zotero (free)
- EndNote (institutional license may be available)
- Google Scholar citation export
