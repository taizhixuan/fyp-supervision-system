TITLE: Supervisor Selection and Working with Supervisors

CHOOSING THE RIGHT SUPERVISOR
Selecting the right supervisor is one of the most important decisions in your FYP. Consider:

1. RESEARCH AREA ALIGNMENT
- Check the supervisor's research areas and expertise
- Look at their publications and past projects
- Choose someone whose expertise matches your project topic
- A supervisor who knows your domain can provide better guidance

2. AVAILABILITY AND WORKLOAD
- Check the supervisor's current supervision load
- Some supervisors have quotas for how many students they can supervise
- Consider if the supervisor has time for regular meetings
- Check their office hours and availability

3. SUPERVISION STYLE
- Some supervisors are hands-on, others give more independence
- Think about what style works best for you
- Ask current or past students about their experience
- Meet potential supervisors to discuss expectations

4. EXPERTISE IN TOOLS AND TECHNOLOGIES
- A supervisor familiar with your technology stack can help more
- However, don't limit your choice to only this factor
- Subject matter expertise is more important than tool expertise

HOW TO APPROACH A POTENTIAL SUPERVISOR
1. Research their background and interests first
2. Prepare a brief project idea (1-2 paragraphs)
3. Send a formal email introducing yourself and your interest
4. Request a meeting to discuss the possibility
5. Be professional and respectful of their time
6. Have alternative supervisors in mind

WORKING EFFECTIVELY WITH YOUR SUPERVISOR

Expectations from Students:
- Attend all scheduled meetings
- Come prepared with progress and questions
- Submit work on time
- Be honest about challenges
- Act on feedback promptly
- Maintain meeting logs
- Communicate proactively

Expectations from Supervisors:
- Provide regular guidance and feedback
- Review submitted work in reasonable time
- Be available during scheduled meetings
- Help navigate academic requirements
- Provide constructive criticism
- Support your learning and growth

COMMUNICATION BEST PRACTICES
- Use email for formal communication
- Be clear and specific in your messages
- Respond to supervisor emails promptly
- Schedule meetings in advance
- Send meeting agendas before meetings
- Follow up with meeting summaries
- Don't contact supervisors late at night or on holidays unless urgent

DEALING WITH SUPERVISOR CONFLICTS
If issues arise:
1. Communicate your concerns directly and professionally
2. Try to understand the supervisor's perspective
3. If unresolved, speak to the FYP coordinator
4. Document all communications
5. Remain professional throughout
